# The Offer: Look Twice

> **Status:** Rebuilt 2026-06-07 (during Phase 15 discussion). This supersedes the original Phase 14 version, which was structured as a single three-rung commitment ladder. That earlier shape was built on a wrong premise — it was gathered through a constrained single-answer question format that flattened a richer offer into a tidy ascending ladder, before anyone checked how it would influence downstream build work. The real offer is a **catalogue of services across two audiences**, not one ladder.
>
> **Ownership:** Jamie builds and decides for this `new-site` pass; Kris takes over for final voice and content tweaks before launch. Copy and prices below are **provisional working drafts** marked `[PROVISIONAL]`. The *architecture* is firm; the *words and numbers* are Kris's to finalise.

## What the offer is

A catalogue of services a visitor selects from, split into two audiences:

- **For businesses** — three ways to work with Kris: the connected **Strategy Suite**, à la carte **Strat-bombs** (one-off targeted hits), and an ongoing **Retainer**.
- **For agencies** — availability and pedigree for agency-side support.

It is **not** a Good/Better/Best pricing table, and it is **not** a single climb-the-rungs ladder. Each service is a different kind of engagement. Not every client does all of them — you pick what fits your problem. The way in for every paid service is the same: a free 30-min call. This is not an e-commerce site; "buy" means "book a call about this specific thing."

**Why it reads as a catalogue, not tiers:** tiers read as tiers because they are the same template at three prices. Here, each service has its own copy, colour, and imagery (accent-on-neutral theming), which breaks the upgrade-tier grammar on its own. Layout, copy, and leading-with-the-entry all reinforce "different engagements for different problems."

## Free Call (the single primary CTA, site-wide)

- **What it is:** a 30-min conversation. Surface-level problem-naming and a read on fit. No pitch.
- **Purpose:** qualification plus a genuinely useful problem-naming conversation. You leave with a clearer view of your situation whether or not you go further.
- **Reassurance line:** "No sale, no follow-up unless you want one." `[PROVISIONAL]`
- **Contact route:** the single Formspree contact form at `#contact`. No mailto, no direct booking.
- **It is the way in for every service. It is not itself a priced service.**

Every service CTA across the catalogue routes here. The button label is generic and consistent (working label "Book a call", final wording TBD in design — Jamie wants to see how busy it reads). When a visitor arrives from a specific service, the contact form shows a contextual state (see "Contact form carry-through" below).

# For businesses

## Block 1: The Strategy Suite (connected sequence)

The structured path. Three rungs shown **as a sequence** (Diagnosis → Strategy → Embedding), with a single section-level CTA. Noun titles. You can start at the rung that fits; not everyone does all three.

### Diagnosis `[PROVISIONAL]`
- **Price:** ~£2,000 fixed
- **Duration:** ~2-3 weeks
- **Outcome (card face):** "Name the real problem, not the symptom, with a clear direction out."
- **Expander (how it works):** initial call and discussion, interview(s) with key stakeholder(s), market research, analysis of current brand communications, report of findings.
- **Output:** written report plus debrief call.

### Strategy `[PROVISIONAL]`
- **Price:** from ~£6,000
- **Duration:** ~4-8 weeks
- **Outcome (card face):** "Turn the diagnosis into a brand-and-experience strategy your team can act on."
- **Expander (how it works):** builds on the Diagnosis (or equivalent existing evidence); structured strategy development (positioning, narrative, experience principles, or CX strategy, scope set before start); stakeholder workshops; strategy document plus implementation framework; presentation to the leadership team; one follow-on call 30 days after delivery.
- **Output:** strategy document, workshop outputs, leadership presentation.

### Embedding `[PROVISIONAL]`
- **Price:** priced per project (scales with implementation scope and time)
- **Outcome (card face):** "Strategy is worth nothing if it stays in the deck. This is the part where it actually happens."
- **Card body:** Kris works alongside your team to put the recommendations into practice. She looks at what you do and what you don't, finds the gaps, answers questions as they land, and supports the rollout on demand. Not consult-and-leave. She stays until the change is real, and measured.
- **Expander (how it works):** hands-on with your team to embed the brand; a review of what you do and don't do, with the gaps named; on-demand support through implementation; results measured.
- **Output:** an implemented strategy — changed marketing, comms, and messaging, and where needed, design and targeting.

**Suite CTA:** one section-level call, e.g. "Want to understand if the full suite is right for you?" → free call.

**Note:** Embedding (a bounded implementation project, with a measured end-state) is distinct from the Retainer (open-ended, on-tap). Enforce the difference in copy and design.

## Block 2: Strat-bombs (product grid)

Targeted one-off communications-strategy hits. A grid of standalone products, each with its own CTA. Buy the one that is your problem. Three at launch; the set will grow over time. The three share method DNA (comms + channel analysis recurs); they are differentiated by the *problem* each solves, so copy keeps them clearly separate.

**Card pattern (all strat-bombs):** the visible face is short and scannable (name, one-line outcome, price, scoped CTA hook + generic button). Depth lives in a keyboard-accessible expander (description, how it works, what you walk away with). Face short, expander deep.

### Market-fit Analysis `[PROVISIONAL]`
- **Price:** £3,500
- **Card face:** "Aiming at the wrong market, or the right market with the wrong message? This finds which, and fixes it."
- **Expander:** A clear read on how your product fits its market, and how your messaging lands. Built from research into your product, brand, market, and competitors.
  - **How it works:** interview stakeholders, understand the product and business, research the market and competitors.
  - **You walk away with:** a working communications guide — who to target, the best channels, and how to talk to them. Practical enough to hand your ad team or feed internal AI tooling. Delivered as a presentation, with up to 3 hours of follow-up.
- **CTA hook:** "Chat to me about market-fit analysis" → [Book a call]

### ROAS booster `[PROVISIONAL]`
- **Price:** £3,500
- **Card face:** "Spending on ads but customers don't stick around? The gap is usually between what your ads promise and what your brand actually delivers."
- **Expander:** A full look at where your experience disconnects: product, brand ethos, comms and channels, design, and your website. Clear recommendations on what to change and where, so your ad spend stops buying one-time customers and starts building advocates. The shift from "I got it off some website" to "you have to try this."
  - **How it works:** review of product, brand ethos, comms style and channel strategy, design, and website experience. Recommendations as a working guide, played back in a presentation with follow-up.
- **CTA hook:** "Chat to me about boosting your ROAS" → [Book a call]

### CX alignment guide `[PROVISIONAL]`
- **Price:** £7,000 (heaviest of the three — includes first-party customer research)
- **Card face:** "What you think your brand is, versus what your customers actually experience. This finds the gap, in their words."
- **Expander:** Starts with your intent — the brand and experience you mean to deliver. Then it goes to your customers to find out what they actually get: first and second-hand research, interviews, surveys. You get a clear map of where intent and reality pull apart, and what to do to close it.
  - **How it works:** understand your intended brand and experience, then research the real one (customer interviews, surveys, plus comms and channel analysis). Recommendations as a working guide and presentation.
  - **Optional add-on:** ongoing consultancy to help you embed the changes (a natural bridge to the Retainer / Embedding).
- **CTA hook:** "Chat to me about aligning your CX" → [Book a call]

## Block 3: Retainer (single offer, three usage levels)

Kris on tap. A senior strategy partner, monthly, flexible and prioritised. Pick a monthly level and it goes to work on what matters most, flexing month to month between quick hits, strategy, and hands-on embedding. The three levels are framed as **roles** (how you use Kris), not amounts — escalating involvement, not Good/Better/Best.

**Working title:** "Retainer" (Kris can name it better). **Tier labels are working models, `[PROVISIONAL]` — Jamie wants more personality / tongue-in-cheek, Kris may differ.**

- **£2,000/mo · Advisor** `[PROVISIONAL]` — on call for advice and the lighter one-shots.
- **£4,000/mo · Consultant** `[PROVISIONAL]` — hands-on with bigger pieces and projects, ongoing input.
- **£6,000/mo · Team member** `[PROVISIONAL]` — the most embedded: like having Kris on the team, priority access, the big work.

**Logic:** more money = more activity. Higher-value work lives in higher levels (a low retainer is unlikely to fund CX alignment, which is ~3.5 months of the £2k level). More embedding and more availability at higher levels.

**Mechanics:** 3-month minimum, rolling monthly after. Discounts for longer commitments.

**Prioritisation:** each month is shaped together — what you want, and what Kris thinks you actually need. A partner who steers, not a menu you order from.

**Timeline visual (design):** a 6-month example showing the mix flex — months 1: a couple of one-shots · 2-3: discovery + strategy · 4-6: embedding + consult. The point it makes: *flexible, and prioritised around you.*

## Contact form carry-through

Each service CTA carries its product context to the contact form at `#contact`. On arrival from a service, the form shows a contextual state:

- "I'm interested in: [service name]" rendered as a small visual echo of the card (the mini-card, a design-step element).
- "Optional: anything you'd like to add?" — a free-text field, **not** required. The selected service is the message.
- The selected service is included in the Formspree submission (hidden field).
- **Default state** (arriving from a non-service CTA — nav, suite section CTA): blank "I'm interested in" with a placeholder; visitor types freely.

This modifies the existing contact section (`initContactForm`, `js/main.js`), it does not just link to it.

## Price formats (for the card system)

The repeatable card system must handle these price-display variants:
- **Fixed** — "£3,500", "~£2,000 fixed"
- **From** — "from ~£6,000"
- **Per month** — "£2,000/mo"
- **Per project** — "Priced per project"
- **Hidden** — no price shown (agency)

VAT: a single "All costs exclude VAT" line at the **section end**, not per card.

## Build steer (for the planner)

These are **starter services that evolve over time** — products will be added, removed, and reordered. Build a **data-driven, repeatable card/block system**, not hand-built bespoke blocks. The system needs four block types:
1. **Connected-sequence block** (the Suite — N rungs with a through-line, one shared CTA)
2. **Product-grid block** (Strat-bombs — N one-offs, per-card CTA + hook)
3. **Single-offer-with-levels block** (the Retainer — one offer, three usage levels, + timeline visual)
4. **Single-offer, price-hidden block** (Agency work)

Some future services may chain into a sequence, some stand alone — the block types should not assume a fixed count or a fixed order.
