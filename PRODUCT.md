# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

**Primary - SMEs and scale-ups** whose ambition has outgrown their brand or experience.
Leaders who want a strategic partner, not a supplier who waits to be briefed.

Decision-makers: founders, MDs/CEOs, Heads of Brand/Marketing/CX, Brand/Marketing Directors.

Five trigger situations that define who is a good fit (situation matters more than
demographics):

- **The Outgrown Founder** - business has grown faster than the brand. Something is off
  but nobody can name it precisely. Trigger: growth milestone, rebrand consideration,
  new market entry.
- **The Inherited Strategy Sceptic** - new leader, inherited a brand strategy they did
  not build and do not believe in. It exists on paper but is not driving decisions or
  behaviour. Trigger: new hire, post-acquisition, leadership change.
- **The Operations-First Leader** - built the business around operational excellence,
  but the brand has not kept up. The experience is good; the story told about it is
  weak or inconsistent. Marketing investment not delivering; premium pricing not sticking.
- **The Post-Rebrand Disappointment** - rebrand completed, looks better, nothing has
  changed. The new brand is not showing up in the actual experience and nobody knows why.
  Trigger: post-rebrand review, six to twelve months after launch.
- **The Agency Borrower** (secondary) - agency or consultancy with a complex brief
  spanning brand, CX, and comms. Needs senior strategy firepower without a full-time hire.

**What makes them pick up the phone - their language, not ours:**
"We have just rebranded but nothing feels different." / "Marketing and ops are always
arguing about whose fault it is." / "Customers love the product but hate dealing with
us." / "We know what we stand for, we just cannot seem to get everyone to act like it."

**Good-fit mindset:** wants to understand the real problem, not validate a decision
already made. Willing to hear uncomfortable truths. Has enough authority to act on what
is found. Sees strategy as investment, not a cost to minimise.

**Red flags:** has already decided the answer and wants strategy to justify it.
Organisation too siloed to act on cross-functional recommendations.

## Product Purpose

looktwice.uk is a **proof and credibility asset** in a largely relationship-led BD
process. At launch, the site is not expected to generate cold inbound - its job is to be
coherent and credible when a warm contact or referral looks it up.

Visitor journey: arrive from LinkedIn or word of mouth, quickly assess whether she
understands their world, decide whether to make contact.

They should leave thinking: "she is describing the problems I have, even if I did not
have the words for them - and I know what my business would get from working with her."

Primary CTA: a free 30-45 minute problem identification session. Low commitment.
Clear value to the prospect regardless of whether they proceed.

Secondary goal: search and LLM discoverability for brand strategy, CX strategy, and
brand-CX alignment terms.

V1 priority: vital information fast, zero friction to contact.
Blog, newsletter, and interactive features are V2+.

**Services direction:** three areas (brand strategy, CX and experience strategy,
research and insight), likely framed as packages with problem-first positioning rather
than discipline descriptions. Offer structure is still being finalised - treat as
directional.

## Brand Personality

Curious, energetic, direct, pragmatic.

Confident but not corporate. Intellectually sharp but not cold. A bit of edge,
still professional.

Voice: clear, human, strategically opinionated. Short sentences. Plain English.
No fluff. Witty in small doses - dry or observational, never forced. Honest about
trade-offs and tensions.

The test: does it sound like someone who thinks clearly, questions lazily-accepted
ideas, and is not afraid to say the uncomfortable thing out loud - without being
abrasive or performative?

## Anti-references

- Generic solo brand and strategy consultant sites - same AI-generated positioning,
  no personality, no point of view
- Corporate consultancy tone - no humanity, takes itself too seriously, trying to
  look clever
- "We help brands grow" filler copy that says nothing
- Stock photography used without visual consistency
- Sites that make the visitor work to understand what you actually do
- Luxury serif, high-polish aesthetic (wrong register - suggests unaffordable,
  not right for the SME and scale-up audience)
- Identical card grids, decorative motion, heavy scroll burden over substance

## Design Principles

1. **Practice what you preach.** A consultancy about brand-CX alignment whose own site
   does not embody its brand is the first thing a sharp prospect notices. The site must
   be the demonstration, not just the description.

2. **Help them name the problem before offering the solution.** Clients arrive with
   symptoms, not briefs - "we just rebranded and nothing changed", not "I need
   brand-CX integration." Surface the recognisable situations first. Let people
   self-identify. Services and packages follow from that, not the other way around.

3. **Directness as respect.** The audience is time-poor and sceptical of consultant
   waffle. No hedging, no jargon, no intros that repeat the heading. Every word earns
   its place.

4. **Earned credibility, not stated capability.** "12 years experience" is forgettable.
   The case study structure - Brief / Real Problem / Strategy / Impact - is proof.
   Show the diagnosis. The CV is secondary.

5. **Personality is non-negotiable.** The risk of blending in is exactly what she
   diagnoses in clients. The site should be visibly, unmistakably Kris - curious,
   energetic, direct. If it could have been made by any solo consultant, it has failed.

## Accessibility and Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum. Motion is welcome when it aids understanding or information delivery -
not for decorative transitions. Alt text on all images. Screenreader compatible
throughout.
